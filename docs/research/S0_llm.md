# Indexing LLM selection for a local LightRAG context kernel

## TL;DR

For this specific workload, the best fit today is **Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507** in **GGUF Q4_K_M** on **llama.cpp ROCm**. It lines up unusually well with LightRAG’s own guidance for indexing—**at least 32B**, **at least 32K context**, and **avoid reasoning models during document indexing**—while also removing your highest-risk failure mode because it is **non-thinking only** and officially does **not** emit `<think>` blocks. Its Q4_K_M quant is also comfortably inside your practical VRAM envelope at **18.63GB on disk**. citeturn13view0turn35view4turn25search2

The strongest runner-up is **Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B**, but I would only choose it if you are willing to treat ingest as an engineering project rather than a drop-in model swap. It is **thinking-on by default**, does **not** support the `/think` / `/nothink` soft switch, has an open **llama.cpp** issue about answers appearing inside think tags, and its mainstream Q4_K_M GGUF is **22.29GB**, which is over your stated comfort zone before you even reserve headroom for embeddings and KV slack. citeturn33view2turn16view0turn25search1

## Best overall pick

The single best indexing LLM under your constraints is **Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507**. The shortest way to explain the choice is this: **LightRAG itself says indexing wants a non-reasoning model with at least 32B parameters and at least 32K context**, and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 is the candidate that most cleanly satisfies all three constraints at once while still fitting your GPU budget. It is a **30.5B / 3.3B-active MoE**, it has a **262K native context window**, it is **non-thinking-only**, and its **Q4_K_M** quant is **18.63GB** rather than the 22GB class you run into with Qwen3.6. citeturn13view0turn35view2turn35view4turn25search2

The runner-up is **Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B**, but the trade-off is unfavorable for an offline structured-extraction engine. Qwen3.6’s official card says it **thinks by default**, does **not** support the old Qwen3 soft switch, and requires serving-side configuration to force a direct response. On top of that, **llama.cpp issue #22398** documents answers appearing in think tags, and the best public workaround I found is **latest llama.cpp + `--reasoning off`**, with an additional community-fixed template project trying to make the no-reasoning path more robust. That is workable for bench testing; it is not the level of deterministic boringness I would want for a LightRAG ingest that can silently fail. citeturn33view2turn16view0turn18view0turn19search7

The other big reason to prefer Qwen3-30B-Instruct-2507 is that the public evidence I found for **actual extraction-step use** tilts toward it, not Qwen3.6. A public GraphRAG-on-LightRAG discussion reports **decent results using `qwen3:30b-a3b-instruct-2507-q4_K_M` for the extraction step**, and I found a LightRAG example script configured with **`Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507`** as the indexing model. I did **not** find comparably direct public LightRAG extraction-success evidence for Qwen3.6. citeturn34search1turn34search5

If you want a non-Qwen challenger, the model I would keep in reserve is **GLM-4.7-Flash**, not because it is safer, but because it is the one same-class 30B-A3B model that looks genuinely competitive on public agentic benchmarks. I still would not choose it first for delimiter-safe indexing because its public local-deployment story is less settled, its official structured-output metrics are less transparent, and there were early local-format / looping complaints around its GGUFs and parsers. citeturn9view0turn20search6turn25search14turn20search28

## Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 versus Qwen3.6-35B-A3B

Your stated premise that both models are “about **17GB at Q4_K_M**” does **not** hold for the mainstream llama.cpp GGUFs I found. For **Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507**, the common **Q4_K_M** file is **18.63GB**. For **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B**, the common **Q4_K_M** file is **22.29GB**, with **Q4_K_S** still at **21.49GB** and **Q4_0** at **20.84GB**. Under your stated goal of leaving **3–4GB** for a co-resident embedder and KV slack, that means Qwen3.6 is not merely “a little larger”; it is a **budget inversion**. citeturn25search2turn25search1

On think-tag leakage, I would **confirm the issue exists in public reports** and **refute the idea that there is already a perfectly clean deterministic fix in stock llama.cpp**. The strongest evidence is open **llama.cpp issue #22398**, which reports Qwen3.6 answers landing in think tags and odd stopping behavior. Qwen’s own official card says **Qwen3.6 thinks by default** and **does not officially support** the `/think` / `/nothink` soft switch. There is a public Hugging Face discussion where a user reports that the latest llama.cpp supports **`--reasoning off`**, and a community “fixed chat templates” project claims it now keeps `--reasoning off` out of error-path think blocks. But that still falls short of “deterministically safe for unattended ingestion.” For your workload, that distinction matters. citeturn16view0turn33view2turn18view0turn19search7

On public LightRAG-style extraction evidence, **Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 has the better paper trail**. I found a public discussion around a local graph-extraction product built on LightRAG saying they had **decent results** with **`qwen3:30b-a3b-instruct-2507-q4_K_M`** for extraction, and I found a LightRAG example script configured with **Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507**. For **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B**, I did **not** find a similarly explicit public report of successful LightRAG-style entity extraction in the sources I checked. citeturn34search1turn34search5

On official structure-adjacent metrics, **Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507** is easy to quote: the official model card reports **IFEval 84.7** and **BFCL-v3 65.1**. For **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B**, I did **not** find text-parsed official IFEval/BFCL numbers in the sources I checked, so I do **not** want to invent a number from an inaccessible benchmark image. citeturn35view4

My recommendation between the two is therefore straightforward: **choose Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 unless you have already proven, on your own prompt and delimiter harness, that Qwen3.6 with `--reasoning off` never leaks think tags and that the lower-fit quant you need still preserves extraction precision**. As of **May 24, 2026**, I would not make that bet for a silent-ingest pipeline. citeturn35view4turn16view0turn25search1

## Newer challengers in the same deployment class

The most relevant **Qwen Coder** model I found is **Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct**. On paper, it is attractive for your use case: same **30.5B / 3.3B-active** class as Qwen3-30B-Instruct-2507, same **262K** context, and—crucially—**non-thinking only**, with a deliberately structured function-call format. That means it deserves a serious A/B test if you find Qwen3-30 occasionally deviates from LightRAG’s delimiter grammar. The caveat is domain fit: Qwen3-Coder is optimized for coding/tool workflows, while your workload is **English technical prose extraction**, not tool calling or code generation, and I found no direct LightRAG extraction evidence for it. citeturn10view1turn10view2

I also checked **Qwen3-Coder-Next**, but it is not a real fit for your box. Its own model card lists **80B total / 3B activated** with **262K** native context and non-thinking behavior, but that 80B total-parameter footprint pushes it well outside a sane **~20GB** GGUF budget on a single **7900 XTX**. In other words, it is architecturally interesting and likely excellent for structured tool use, but **not for this deployment envelope**. citeturn10view6turn10view7turn33view7

Outside the Qwen family, **GLM-4.7-Flash** is the most credible same-class challenger I found. The official card describes it as a **30B-A3B MoE**, and Z.ai’s own benchmark table shows it outperforming **Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking-2507** on several agentic and browsing-style tasks, including **τ²-Bench** and **BrowseComp**. Community evidence also shows it being run locally with **llama.cpp**-style GGUFs and benchmarked on **7900 XTX** ROCm/Vulkan setups. The reason I still do **not** rank it above Qwen3-30B-Instruct-2507 for your use case is that I found **less transparent structure-oriented benchmarking** and **more local-format turbulence** around parser/model-format bugs and early looping issues. citeturn9view0turn20search1turn20search6turn25search14turn20search28

I did **not** find a qualifying **DeepSeek V3.x / V4.x** open-weight model in the **≤35B MoE / ≤4B-active** class that cleanly replaces Qwen3-30 or Qwen3.6 for this deployment. What I did surface were the large frontier **DeepSeek-V3.2** pages and older dense distillation artifacts, not a small open-weight MoE directly comparable to the Qwen A3B class on your hardware. Likewise, the **GLM-4.5-Air** family is much larger at **106B total / 12B active**, which misses your preferred active-parameter class, and the recent **Mistral Small 4** / **Devstral 2** family entries are **119B / 123B**-class, again outside your envelope. IBM’s current **Granite 4.1** family is explicitly **dense**—**3B, 8B, and 30B**—not Granite MoE. citeturn9view1turn22search24turn22search8turn23view3

My practical conclusion is conservative: **I do not see a 2026 release in this class that clearly beats Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 on the combination of local fit, no-think safety, and delimiter-risk management**. The best challengers are **Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct** if you want to bias toward structured tool-like outputs, and **GLM-4.7-Flash** if you are willing to tolerate a riskier local stack. citeturn10view1turn10view2turn9view0

## Whether a dense fallback is worth it

The high-level answer is **no**: I do **not** see evidence that any dense model so overwhelmingly dominates the MoE candidates on structured output that the throughput/quality trade is worth it for **~1000 indexing calls**. LightRAG’s own README is a strong clue here: the project warns that indexing is substantially more demanding than ordinary RAG because the LLM must perform entity-relation extraction, and it recommends **at least 32B parameters** while also advising against reasoning models during indexing. That recommendation does **not** prove that every smaller dense model will fail, but it does argue against making a 7B–14B dense model your default when a fitting A3B MoE already exists. citeturn13view0turn13view1

The call-count math makes big dense models especially unattractive. Using your own rule-of-thumb numbers, if the extraction stage emits an average of **200 tokens** over **1000 calls**, that is **200,000 output tokens**. At **80–100 tok/s** for a small-active-parameter MoE, you are at roughly **33–42 minutes** of pure decode time. At **15–20 tok/s** for a dense ~30B class model, you are at roughly **2.8–3.7 hours**. If the average output is only **100 tokens**, the gap is still roughly **17–21 minutes** versus **1.4–1.9 hours**. That is a very large productivity tax to pay unless the dense model is materially better on delimiter obedience and extraction precision. In the sources I checked, I did **not** find that kind of decisive quality edge. 

If you want a dense fallback anyway, I would recommend **Granite-4.1-8B** before I would recommend a 70B-class quant or an older dense 14B as the operational fallback. That may sound counterintuitive, but IBM’s official card gives you the best structure-oriented evidence I found among the dense models—**IFEval 87.06**, **BFCL v3 68.27**—and explicitly names **text extraction** and **RAG** as intended uses, all under **Apache 2.0** with a very small GGUF footprint. The trade-off is obvious: it is still an **8B dense model**, and LightRAG itself wants **≥32B** for indexing. So I would treat Granite not as a superior replacement for Qwen3-30, but as the **best dense safety fallback** if you decide that operational simplicity matters more than top-end extraction quality. citeturn23view3turn24view3turn24view4

If you want the larger dense fallback instead, **Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct** is the one I would pick over Gemma-3-12B-it. The reason is not that Qwen2.5 is unquestionably “smarter”; it is that its official model card makes a direct claim that matters for your pipeline—improvements in **structured outputs especially JSON**, long-input behavior, and robustness to differing system prompts. That is closer to LightRAG’s actual failure mode than generic chat benchmarks. But again, I would frame Qwen2.5-14B as a **fallback baseline**, not the default winner. citeturn29view2turn29view3

## LightRAG-specific footguns

- **Reasoning models during indexing are the biggest format hazard.** LightRAG explicitly recommends **not** using reasoning models at the document-indexing stage, and real LightRAG issues show why: one issue reports that models with only a closing `</think>` tag broke LightRAG’s think-tag removal logic, and another reports retrieval truncation when stored chunks contain `<think>...</think>`. For an extractor whose output must match a strict delimiter grammar, that is a red flag, not a cosmetic annoyance. citeturn13view0turn14view1turn14view2

- **Silent extraction failure is real.** LightRAG issue **#30** documents an ingest path where documents were chunked successfully but **no entities or relationships** were extracted with some Ollama-backed local models. That is the exact class of failure your workload should guard against. I would add a pre-ingest harness that validates a few dozen representative chunks and rejects any model/template/settings combo that fails to emit exact delimiter-conforming records. citeturn14view0

- **For Qwen3.6 specifically, “disable reasoning” is still not the same thing as “guaranteed no think leakage.”** The official card says Qwen3.6 thinks by default and does not support `/think` / `/nothink`; a Hugging Face discussion says **`--reasoning off`** works in the latest llama.cpp; but there is also an open **llama.cpp** bug about answers in think tags. For unattended indexing, that means Qwen3.6 needs a stricter canary test than Qwen3-30B-Instruct-2507. citeturn33view2turn18view0turn16view0

- **Stay conservative on GGUF quants for indexing.** I did **not** find a published LightRAG-specific study comparing extraction quality across GGUF quant levels, so my recommendation is operational: for indexing, prefer the vendor/GGUF authors’ “default quality” points—typically **Q4_K_M** or better—and resist the temptation to save another couple of gigabytes with very aggressive low-bit quants. In a knowledge graph, a small speed win is often less valuable than avoiding polluted entities and malformed relation records. The GGUF repos I checked consistently label **Q4_K_M** or similar as the default “good quality” choice. citeturn25search2turn25search1turn26search3turn27search6

- **Use the model-family’s low-entropy non-thinking settings, not creative-chat defaults.** Qwen3-30B-Instruct-2507’s own best-practices section recommends **temperature 0.7, top_p 0.8, top_k 20, min_p 0** for instruct mode, while warning that higher presence penalties can reduce repetition but may also cause language mixing. Qwen3.6’s non-thinking guidance is also distinctly lower-entropy than its thinking defaults. For extraction, I would start even more conservatively than the chat defaults and optimize only after measuring delimiter fidelity. citeturn35view3turn33view2

- **Model-specific parser/template mismatches are not hypothetical.** For **Mistral Small 3.1**, there is a public discussion about a **Mistral-Common vs Hugging Face tokenizer mismatch**. For **GLM-4.7-Flash**, community GGUF discussions describe early parser/model-family misidentification and a separate note says updated GGUFs were needed after a **llama.cpp** bug caused looping and poor outputs. These are exactly the kinds of issues that show up as delimiter corruption in a graph extractor. citeturn22search17turn25search14turn20search28
