# Local Embedder Selection for a LightRAG Context Kernel

## TL;DR

The best single embedder for your setup is **Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B**, served through **sentence-transformers on PyTorch ROCm** rather than through GGUF unless you specifically validate the GGUF path yourself. It is the strongest sub-4 GB option I could verify on the metrics that matter to you: **61.83 on MTEB English Retrieval**, **32K context**, **asymmetric instruction-aware retrieval**, and an **Apache-2.0** license. citeturn45view0turn28view0

The main trade-off is deployment smoothness. **bge-m3** remains the more battle-tested “boring default” in local RAG ecosystems and does not require query instructions, but Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B buys you a longer context window and a meaningfully stronger retrieval profile for English RAG workloads. The catch is that the **llama.cpp / GGUF path has had correctness and speed footguns**, so the safest recommendation is ROCm PyTorch first, GGUF second. citeturn42view0turn52search0turn52search9

## Best fit under your constraints

Under your exact constraints, the best single choice is **Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B**. On the benchmark slice you actually care about, it posts **61.83 on MTEB English Retrieval**, ahead of **gte-Qwen2-1.5B-instruct at 50.25** and well ahead of the older LLM-style 1.5B GTE option, while also giving you **32K context** and explicit **instruction-aware asymmetric retrieval**. Those are the right axes for long English technical docs in a graph-RAG kernel. citeturn45view0turn28view0

The runner-up I would keep in reserve is **bge-m3**, not because it is the better fit on paper, but because it is the safer fallback if you want the fewest moving parts. It is long-context at **8192 tokens**, MIT-licensed, widely integrated, does **not** require query instruction prompting for dense retrieval, and it is exactly the family LightRAG users tend to start from. The trade-off I am explicitly accepting by preferring Qwen3-0.6B is **a little more integration fragility in exchange for materially better retrieval and a much longer context window**. citeturn42view0turn39search5turn45view0

I would not make **gte-Qwen2-1.5B-instruct** the default for this system. It barely squeaks under your budget in FP16, needs `trust_remote_code`, has query-side instruction requirements, and still trails Qwen3-0.6B by a wide margin on the retrieval slice surfaced in the Qwen comparison table. If you want a “smaller than Qwen3-4B but stronger than classic BERT encoders” fallback, it is defensible, but it is not the best answer here. citeturn44view0turn45view0turn52search19

## Whether the 4B jump is worth it

The benchmark answer is straightforward: **Qwen3-Embedding-4B scores 68.46 on MTEB English Retrieval**, versus **61.83 for the 0.6B model**, for a gain of **6.63 points** on retrieval. If you were choosing in a vacuum, that is a real improvement rather than noise. citeturn45view0

For your specific machine budget, though, the recommendation is **no**. A 4B model is roughly **8 GB in FP16** and roughly **4 GB at Q8**, before you account for runtime overhead, allocator fragmentation, and the fact that you are already keeping a **17–20 GB quantized MoE LLM** resident on the same 24 GB card. In practice, that means the 4B embedder consumes essentially the entire budget you reserved for embeddings, leaving very little safety margin. By contrast, Qwen3-0.6B is roughly **1.2 GB FP16** or **0.6 GB Q8**, which is much more compatible with co-residency. The 4B variant is only worth it if you are willing to split duties across devices, unload the extraction model during indexing, or otherwise relax the co-resident constraint. citeturn28view1turn28view0

If you want the shortest concrete recommendation: **use Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B now; do not spend the VRAM on Qwen3-Embedding-4B for this box.** The retrieval gain is real, but the capacity hit is wrong for the system you described. citeturn45view0turn28view1

## Footguns and things I couldn't confirm

The important **LightRAG footguns** for the recommended model are all operational. **Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B expects asymmetric query formatting**. In sentence-transformers, the intended path is `prompt_name="query"` on the query side and plain encoding on the passage side; in raw transformers, Qwen’s own template is `Instruct: ...\nQuery: ...`. Qwen explicitly says that **not using an instruction on the query side can cost about 1% to 5% retrieval performance**, so if your LightRAG embed hook encodes raw user queries without the prefix, you are likely leaving accuracy on the table. This is an inference about framework wiring, but the model requirement itself is explicit. citeturn28view0turn45view0

The second footgun is **pooling and normalization**. Qwen’s reference code uses **left padding**, **last-token pooling**, and then **L2 normalization** before similarity search. If you bypass sentence-transformers and wire the raw model into LightRAG yourself, those three details matter. Treat them as part of the model contract, not optional cleanup. citeturn28view0turn45view0

The third footgun is the **GGUF path**. Official GGUFs exist for Qwen3 embeddings, and llama.cpp clearly supports embedding mode for the family, but there were at least three red flags in the public issue trail: a **June 2025 bad-output report** for Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B GGUF in llama.cpp, a **February 2026 speed complaint** saying the Qwen embedder path was much slower than similar-sized embedders, and a **March 2026 KoboldCpp ROCm report** where a supposedly CPU-hosted Q8 embedder still tried to allocate about **4.9 GiB** on the AMD GPU when loaded alongside a generation model. That is why my recommendation is **PyTorch ROCm first, GGUF only after local validation**. citeturn52search0turn52search9turn52search12

The main runner-up, **gte-Qwen2-1.5B-instruct**, has its own footguns. It needs `trust_remote_code`, it is **query-instruction-only** rather than symmetric, and there is a model discussion noting a **tokenizer `padding_side` mismatch** depending on how it is loaded. That is survivable, but it is not “drop-in boring.” citeturn44view0turn52search19

The biggest thing I could **not** fully confirm is what you explicitly asked for on every row: a **current live-leaderboard Retrieval-only score and score date** for every candidate directly from the active HF leaderboard surface. The reason is that the old raw JSON files are now **outdated**, and the MTEB maintainers themselves point people toward the **new result cache / benchmark APIs** to reproduce the live numbers. In accessible search/browser surfaces, some models exposed clean retrieval rows and some did not. I therefore marked several rows as **unverified** or **snapshot-based** rather than fabricating precision. citeturn43search0turn38search6

The other unresolved items are narrower. I did not directly verify a **stable ROCm/AMD success report** for every non-Qwen candidate. I also did not directly verify **GGUF availability** for every encoder-style model. And for some models, especially **gte-Qwen2-1.5B-instruct** and **nomic-ai/modernbert-embed-base**, the exact **first-release date** or the precise **max-sequence field** was not cleanly exposed in the source surface I could access.
